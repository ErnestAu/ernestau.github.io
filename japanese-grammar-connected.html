How one word carries tense. Two things to know cold: which of three systems a word belongs to, and the eight slots every word fills.

There are three systems, not five

Every word class conjugates by one of three patterns. Nouns and な-adjectives are literally identical, so learning one gives you both.

Verbs

Godan and ichidan differ only in how you reach the stem. Once you have the stem, the endings are the same.

書く · 食べる
い-adjectives

Their own island. The い carries the tense. です is a politeness tag bolted on the end and never conjugates.

寒い · 高い · ない · たい
Nouns and な-adjectives

The same pattern, exactly. だ／だった plain, です／でした polite. This is the only place でした belongs.

学生 · 静か

There is no such thing as a う-adjective. The う／る split applies to verbs only. Adjectives split into い and な.

Reaching た and て

Ichidan verbs drop る and never change sound. Godan verbs change sound by their final kana - the same change for both た and て, so memorise it once and swap た↔て, だ↔で.

ends inpastて-formexample
う · つ · るったって買う → 買った → 買って
ぬ · ぶ · むんだんで飲む → 飲んだ → 飲んで
くいたいて書く → 書いた → 書いて
ぐいだいで泳ぐ → 泳いだ → 泳いで
すしたして話す → 話した → 話して

行く is irregular here: 行った・行って, not ×いいた. And a verb ending in る is not automatically ichidan - if the vowel before る is a, u or o it's always godan; if i or e it's usually ichidan, except 帰る・入る・走る・知る・切る・要る.
